3. Capital Distribution & Deal Size Stratification

A granular examination of deal sizes reveals a distinct bimodal distribution in private financing. The early-stage seed foundation remains voluminous but dollar-lean, while the upper quartile demonstrates extreme power-law concentration.

Transaction Stratification by Capital Volume 1,778 Included Records

The Early-Stage Majority: Exactly 50.5% of all surveyed transactions (898 companies) raised less than $3.0 million. This bracket represents early-stage technical founders operating on pre-seed and seed rounds, predominantly utilizing Simple Agreements for Future Equity (SAFEs) and convertible notes to minimize immediate governance dilution.

The Institutional Upper Tier: Conversely, records with reported amounts exceeding $25.0 million accounted for just 12.4% of the included records (221 records), yet absorbed $27.61 billion—representing 80.4% of reported capital. The majority of these capital-intensive rounds were concentrated in compute infrastructure, specialized semiconductor tooling, and late-stage clinical therapeutics.

4. Geographic Concentration & Capital Density

Despite national dispersion post-2020, private placement filings in 2026 illustrate a pronounced capital re-clustering around established technical epicenters, driven by dense engineering talent networks and concentrated LP capital.

6. From Public Filing to Usable Prospect List

Why the organization matters

Under Rule 503 of Regulation D, private issuers generally submit Form D notices within fifteen calendar days after the first sale. The filing is public, but the raw source is not a ready-to-use business-development list: names, amounts, industries, locations, amendments, and contact fields still need to be normalized and reviewed.

This export does that structuring work. A recruiter, advisory firm, or technical consultancy can filter by geography, sector, financing size, and filing date, then decide which companies merit outreach. It is a finished research asset—not a claim of exclusive access to SEC data.

7. What the $249 Export Contains

Built for prospecting

Legal entity, state, filing and sale dates, industry grouping, amount sold, security type, executive signer, and available office contact fields—organized for filtering and review.

Clear source boundaries

The aggregate reflects reported amount sold in the included filings. Form D notices can be amended, may precede a completed offering, and do not require a final closeout filing; records should be treated as reported disclosures, not audited final raises.

Cleaning and review

Duplicate entities and inconsistent labels are normalized where identifiable. Amendments and filing-level history remain part of the research context so users can inspect the underlying disclosure.

A practical starting point

The export is designed to save hours of collection and spreadsheet preparation. It does not promise that every company is actively hiring, seeking vendors, or reachable by phone.
